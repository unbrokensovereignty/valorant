# Changelog

Every version of Playbook By Sovereign and what it changed.

## 0.2.5 (in progress)

- **Kits by map.** A kit now counts for more or less depending on the map, side and site, for the reasons a coach would give, and a comp's pros and cons say why. Hush's Telekinesis works Lotus's and Ascent's doors from range, which makes her one of the best duelists on Lotus attack; Displacement only concusses enemies pulled into a wall, so she's better on tight maps than on open ones. Raze's, Breach's and Phoenix's utility needs corners, Cypher's Trapwires and Deadlock's Barrier Mesh need chokes, Sova's darts see little in walled-in sites, Brimstone's smokes don't reach across Breeze or Abyss, and a trap on one of Bind's teleporter exits catches whoever comes through.
- **How each map plays.** Each map's page says whether it's tight, open or in between, what its doors, teleporters, ropes, ziplines and breakable walls do, and which kits make the most of them. Below the best comps, tips for the agents pros pick there, like where Viper walls on Icebox or how Hush uses Lotus's doors.
- **Map by map, for each agent.** An agent's page lists their tips for each map and where their kit does more or less.
- **Fairer to sentinels.** A sentinel on a map where pros mostly play without one no longer drops to the bottom on defense: Chamber holds Breeze's long angles as a fair pick again.
- **Against their team.** The Planner has a new Their team choice: add the other team's agents, as many as you know, and the comps that answer them rank higher. Each comp then says how it stands against what they bring, like an Operator Chamber, a Killjoy and Cypher setup, a fast Raze and Neon hit or Viper and Brimstone lineups: who in the comp answers it, or how to play around it when the comp has little to answer it. Both teams can still play the same agent. Copy includes it in the plan's text.
- **Hush, reviewed.** Her numbers are a little lower everywhere, with her kit adding back where it works best, so she no longer rates near the top on open maps like Breeze.
- **One controller below Immortal.** Two controllers pay off only when a team plays its smokes together, so a second one now costs a comp at most ranks, less at each rank up and nothing from Immortal, and what two controllers add together counts the same way, as does the second controller in what pros run on maps like Lotus. At Platinum, Lotus's best comps lead with one controller on attack, on defense and for both sides, instead of Omen and Clove or the pros' Omen and Viper; pro teams' double controllers come back at the top ranks, and a comp with two says what the second one costs. Viper still leads Breeze from Gold, since one Toxic Screen does the work of several smokes there.
- **Picks for your random teammates.** A duo or trio's row now shows your party with the best agents for your random teammates to play around it, instead of the team you'd most likely get. A Chamber and Omen duo on Lotus attack at Platinum is told Raze, Hush and Fade, where it used to show a likely Clove beside Omen. Open the row to see how the team fits with those picks, what to expect if your teammates pick on their own, other good picks for them and the team they'd most likely pick. The role to ask them for is always one the picks play, and Copy lists the picks too.
- **A rank for every answer.** All ranks is gone from the Planner and Rankings: it gave the same answers as Platinum, since a team plays differently at each rank and the middle one stood in for all of them. Rankings starts on your rank, and a question or saved plan made for all ranks now opens at Platinum, with the same answers as before.
- **How you play.** The first time Playbook opens, it asks your rank, how you queue and the agents you play, and the Planner and Rankings start from the answers. Settings has a new You section to change them, and anyone who used Playbook before is offered the same on Home.
- **Solo.** The Planner plans for one player too. With four random teammates, it ranks the agent for you by every team you could end up with, and shows the best agents for your teammates to play around you; add what they've locked in and it fills in around them. Solo has its own tips, like picking late.
- **Minimaps that match the game.** Every callout on all 13 minimaps was checked against the map, and the ones the game's data puts in the wrong spot have moved: Lotus's C Mound now sits on the mound instead of in a wall, and on Pearl, Sunset and Breeze, where many names were off, every callout is where players stand. A play's routes now follow the corridors and doorways instead of cutting straight through walls.
- **Your agents.** With the agents you play chosen, every plan gives you one of them (a switch under Party turns it off for one question), Rankings marks them with a star, and Home shows how each fits at your rank and their best maps. A new party starts with them for you.
- **How each comp wins.** An opened comp starts with a line on how it wins, from what it does best in the situation and the abilities that do it: on Breeze attack at Immortal, "Wins by smoking off angles with Omen's Dark Cover, finding defenders with Sova's Recon Bolt and winning fights with Neon's Overdrive, then stopping the defuse with Viper's Snake Bite." It never names something the comp is short of, and Copy includes it.
- **Why not this agent?** On the Planner's Agents tab, opening an agent says why their best comp ranks below the best one: which agents it swaps, where it falls short (fewer flashes from Vyse's Arc Rose, say, or pro teams picking Omen more on the map) and where it does better, all read off the same score.
- **A plan on paper.** Print, beside Copy on every comp and saved plan, puts the plan on one sheet of A4 or Letter: each agent with their own tip, how the comp wins, its pros and cons, the play you chose on its Plays tab drawn on the minimap with who does what, and the other tips. A plan for both sides gets a play for each, on a second page if it needs one, and a saved plan prints with its name and note. It's always dark ink on white, whatever your theme. Copy now carries the play you chose too, and a play for each side.
- **Compare two comps.** Compare, on every comp of a five-stack's answers, sets it beside another one (the best other comp to start with, or any you choose): their fits, how each wins, their best plays, how well each covers what the situation needs, and where the lower one falls short or does better, read off the same score.
- **Pistol and eco plays.** Each of the 7 maps in the pool has a play for the pistol round and one for an eco on each side, 28 in all, listed under the other plays: five together through a short way in on attack, close angles and a quick retake on defense, and on an eco, one close fight where a pistol can win it, or three hiding close on one site. Each says what to buy for its round, and Help has a short guide to buying for a pistol round, an eco, a force buy and a full buy.
- **Words that match the kit.** What a comp's cons say to do now names the ability to use ("lean on Omen's Paranoia and close trades"), and never leans on utility the comp is short of. A comp with only a little of something says whose ("Few flashes (only Omen's Paranoia)"), with nothing implying there's more. A play no longer gives a job to an ability that doesn't do it: Veto's Chokehold stands in for flashes and says so, and an agent with nothing for a job says that too. Such a play no longer reads Fits well, however good the rest of the team is at theirs: with one job nobody can do it works at best, and with two it's a stretch, so the plays a comp can run in full come first. Two initiators get a tip about who finds the enemies and who flashes them, from their kits. A Waylay tip on Corrode no longer names Fade when Fade isn't in the comp.
- **Kits, rechecked against the game's ability text.** KAY/O, Fade, Veto, Omen and Clove name the right ability for each thing they do (Clove's Meddle clears corners rather than stunning), Yoru and Omen no longer count as watching the flank, Waylay's Saturate no longer counts as clearing, Viper's kit counts for a little more in solo queue, and KAY/O for a little less.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-29)

- **Answers that fit your rank.** A comp now counts each agent for what a team at your rank gets out of them: a hard or team-dependent kit counts for less at low ranks and in full at Radiant, while gun play counts the same everywhere. Ranked win rates (metabot.gg and winio.ai, October 2026) count too, most in the middle ranks where most games are played, and the pull toward what pro teams play grows mostly from Diamond up. When a comp has an agent who's notably hard to play, like Astra or Yoru, its cons say so from Iron to Platinum, with how much of the kit a team at your rank usually gets.
- **This year's pro play.** Pro picks and comps come from Champions 2026 for the seven maps it played and from Masters Santiago 2026 for Bind, Breeze, Corrode and Pearl, and a map with fewer games behind it pulls less. Each map's pro role mix counts at high ranks: two controllers on Lotus, one on Sunset, and no sentinel on Abyss, Bind, Breeze and Split, where pro teams mostly play without one. A comp far from the mix says so in its cons, and a comp without a sentinel where pros play that way says that too. The Maps page says how many controllers pro teams run on each map, and its line about what wins on a map names the agents pros pick most there this year (Omen and Sova on Ascent, say, where it named Killjoy).
- **Long angles.** A new strength, Operator play, for agents who hold long sightlines with the Operator and have a way out after the shot (Chamber and Jett most of all), and how much each map rewards it. Chamber now shows up in the best comps on the maps where pros pick him.
- **Agent numbers reviewed.** KAY/O brings less information (ZERO/point now only suppresses), Sova's kit pays off with less practice, Brimstone works better alone, and Hush's strengths are reviewed against her released kit. A Clove and Reyna clash with nothing behind it is gone.
- **Agents by rank.** Each agent's page shows their tier at every rank, from Iron to Radiant (your usual rank marked, and a click plans with them there), and their ranked win rate. Their pro pick rate now comes straight from the pro numbers per map.
- **Help explains rank.** A new part of the guide, How your rank changes the answers, says how much of a hard kit a team gets at Iron, where win rates count, and how fast pro play counts as you climb, all from the engine's own numbers. Where the numbers come from names the win-rate sources and this year's events. Duos and trios, in Help and in the Planner's tour, now says rightly that a party's picks are ranked by every team it could end up with, each weighed by how likely it is, not just by the likeliest one.
- **Clearer cons.** A comp missing a role says so once, with how to play around the gaps it leaves, instead of in three separate lines. A gap that someone on the team partly covers names them ("Little flank watch other than Killjoy's"), so it no longer reads as if the team had none.
- **Plays checked.** Two plays no longer smoke the way a teammate is coming in: Lotus's C split leaves C Waterfall open for the pinch, and Fracture's B pincer smokes B Generator instead of B Tunnel, where a teammate holds. With sites chosen, the plays for those sites now come first, as Help says, and the plays for the whole map after them, so Copy gives a play for your site too.
- **Tips for each side.** On defense, a comp's tips now fit defending: the ones for two duelists, controllers or sentinels no longer say to go in together or save smokes for after the plant, and eleven agents whose first tip is about attacking get one for defense instead (Sova's early Recon Bolt, Brimstone's smokes on the choke, Raze's utility against a rush and more). It works the other way too: on attack, Sage, Cypher, Deadlock and Vyse get tips for attacking instead of for holding a site. The new tips are on the agents' pages too. A duo or trio whose other teammates have all locked in no longer gets the tip to ask them for a role.
- **Asking teammates.** A duo or trio's best pick names the role to ask your random teammates for, and now also how often they pick one on their own, so a likely teammate in that role doesn't read as a sure thing. Copy puts that role in the plan's text too, ready to paste into the team chat, along with the fit to expect with random teammates; the party's own fit now says it ranks the party among duos or trios, as the screen does. The line reads right for every role now ("an initiator", not "a initiator").
- **Guided tours.** Help has a new Tours tab: six short tours light up one thing at a time on the real screens, from a three-step First steps to the Planner, Rankings, agent and map pages, and Saved and Settings. Space goes on, ← goes back and Esc stops; finished tours are ticked off, and Home offers the first steps once. They can be started from the search too.
- **Tidier pages.** The Planner's Options no longer shows as an empty heading when a map is chosen, and a map's plays no longer sit right up against its best comps.

## 0.1.5 (2026-10-29)

- **Hush, for real.** Hush is in as a full agent now that she's out: her abilities in the game's own words, her portrait and ability icons, numbers from her first days in ranked and from hands-on reviews of her kit, her best partners and two new tips. She's no longer marked as an estimate, and the Home page and the New tag on her portrait say she's new for her first two months. Pro teams haven't had the chance to pick her yet, so pro play doesn't count for or against her, and her page says her ranked pick rate is from her first days.
- **Patch 13.09.** Every agent's abilities are refreshed from the game's 13.09 text. Ability names stand out, and what an ability does to enemies or for your side is colored, as in the game. Clove's speed boost after Pick-me-up is smaller, as in the patch.
- **Tidier layout.** The role counts under "Roles in the team" line up, and none drops under its name on its own. Dialogs keep their title and buttons in view on short windows, so the agent picker's Done is always in reach. Settings' theme dropdowns no longer stretch, so "All themes" sits beside them, and in a narrow window the section links sit above the settings. Rankings' tabs and role filter stay whole in a narrow window, the Planner's role filter is as wide as its choices, as on Rankings, and an agent's strengths give the abilities more room. The tips for two of a role no longer repeat their heading.
- **Include new agents** in Settings is hidden while no agent is too new for real numbers.

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-29)

The first version.

- **Planner.** Ask for the best comp for a map, side, site and rank, for a five-stack, trio or duo. Fix any part (agents that must be in, agents left out, teammates already locked in, an exact number of a role) and leave the rest open, or ask for the best map, side or rank instead. A duo or trio gets the picks that hold up best whatever random teammates at that rank tend to lock in. When a choice can't fit (a role count for more players than the party has, say), the page says so.
- **Answers you can read.** Every comp comes with a fit from 0 to 100 and a tier from S to D, its strengths and gaps for the situation, its thinnest spot and how to make up for it, the pairs that help or clash, a warning when the map calls for a second controller the comp doesn't have, and how it compares with what pro teams play.
- **Plays and tips.** Hits, splits, defaults, setups and retakes for all 13 maps, filled with the comp's own agents and drawn on the minimap, and tips for the map, side, rank, party and agents. Copy puts a whole plan into words for a chat.
- **Rankings.** Tier lists of agents and comps for any map, side and rank, and every agent's fit on every map in one table.
- **Agents and Maps.** Every agent's kit in the game's own words, strengths, best maps and partners, and every map's sites and callouts on its minimap, with its plays, tips and best comps for each side.
- **Saved.** Keep plans with a note and a star, and save your usual duo, trio or five-stack with the agents each of you plays to plan for it in one click.
- **Hush**, the newest Duelist, is in with early estimates that will be updated once there's play to go on. New agents can be left out of any question.
- **Made to be comfortable.** Ten themes and a theme editor, light or dark following your computer, text sizes, three levels of motion, quiet sounds, search and keys for everything, undo, and backups to a file.
